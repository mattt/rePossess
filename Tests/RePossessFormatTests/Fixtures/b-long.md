## Section 1: Planning the orchard

[1.1] The orchard sits on a narrow lot between two quiet streets, and it gets sun from late morning until evening. Volunteers met on the first Saturday of each month to decide what to do next. Nobody in the group had planted fruit trees before, so every step began with a question. A retired teacher kept the notebook, and she wrote down each decision with the date.

[1.2] The neighbors asked for trees that would not drop fruit onto the sidewalk. We chose dwarf varieties so that children could reach the lowest branches. The soil test showed heavy clay with little organic matter near the surface. Each bed received two wheelbarrows of compost and a thick layer of wood chips.

[1.3] On planting day, twenty people arrived with shovels, gloves, and a large thermos of tea. The holes were dug twice as wide as the root balls and no deeper. A drip line ran along each row, with one emitter on each side of every trunk. In August the group watered twice a week, early in the morning before the heat.

[1.4] Winter pruning removed crossing branches and opened the center of each tree to light. Aphids arrived in spring, and ladybugs arrived a week later to eat them. A family of birds built a nest in the plum tree and stayed until the fruit was ripe. The first harvest was small: eleven pears, a bowl of plums, and one proud apple.

[1.5] We left a basket by the gate with a sign that asked people to take only what they would eat. Next year we will mulch earlier, label every tree, and plant more flowers for the bees. The orchard sits on a narrow lot between two quiet streets, and it gets sun from late morning until evening. Volunteers met on the first Saturday of each month to decide what to do next.

End of section 1.
